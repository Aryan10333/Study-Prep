**Module 03 — Capacity Estimation, Traffic Modeling & Cost Engineering**
- **Formula (core, GPU-count estimation via Little's Law — corrected for internal consistency):** A real, explicit **two-step** derivation, not one flattened ratio, to avoid double-counting concurrency and service time in the same expression. **Step 1 (Little's Law):** real required in-flight concurrency $L = \text{QPS} \times T_{\text{req}}$, where $T_{\text{req}}$ is real mean end-to-end request time (a function of input+output token counts, referencing `06_llm_inference_and_optimization`'s own TTFT/TPOT content, not re-derived) — this step alone answers "how many requests are in flight at once, on average, given this arrival rate and this service time," independent of any per-GPU capacity assumption. **Step 2 (provisioning):** $N_{\text{GPU}} = \left\lceil \dfrac{L}{C_{\text{GPU}} \times U_{\text{target}}} \right\rceil$, where $C_{\text{GPU}}$ is the real, separately-stated max concurrent-request capacity of one GPU replica (a function of that topic's own batching/serving content) and $U_{\text{target}}$ is a real, explicitly stated utilization target (<1, real headroom for traffic variance) — $T_{\text{req}}$ appears only in Step 1, $C_{\text{GPU}}$ only in Step 2, keeping service time and per-GPU concurrency capacity as two distinct, non-overlapping real quantities rather than combined in one ratio.
- **Formula (core, cache savings — cost-basis made explicit per layer, not shared):** $\text{Savings}_{\text{semantic}} = H_{\text{semantic}} \times \text{Cost}_{\text{full-request}}$ (a real semantic-cache hit skips the *entire* downstream pipeline — retrieval and generation both — so its correct cost basis is the full real per-request cost) versus $\text{Savings}_{\text{retrieval}} = H_{\text{retrieval}} \times \text{Cost}_{\text{retrieval-step}}$ (a real retrieval/index-cache hit skips only the real embedding/retrieval lookup — generation still runs, so its correct cost basis is the retrieval step's cost alone, not the full request) — the module states this cost-basis difference explicitly rather than implying both caching layers save against the same real baseline.
- **Hand calc:** A real, fully-worked two-step GPU-count estimate — first computing real required concurrency $L$ via Little's Law from a stated QPS and $T_{\text{req}}$, then provisioning $N_{\text{GPU}}$ from a real, separately-stated $C_{\text{GPU}}$ and $U_{\text{target}}$ — with each step's real numeric result shown before combining them; a second real worked example computing semantic-cache savings against the real full-request cost basis, explicitly contrasted against a separately-computed retrieval-cache savings figure against the real retrieval-step-only cost basis, at a different real, stated hit rate — making the two-caching-layer cost-basis distinction concrete with two separate real numbers computed against two different real baselines, not one shared baseline.

**Module 04 — Data & Knowledge Infrastructure at Scale**
- **Formula (core, storage sizing — replication and index overhead kept as two distinct real factors):** $\text{Storage}_{\text{raw}} = N_{\text{vectors}} \times d_{\text{embed}} \times \text{bytes}_{\text{per-float}}$, then $\text{Storage}_{\text{replicated}} = \text{Storage}_{\text{raw}} \times R$ where $R$ is a real, stated **replication factor** (a genuinely multiplicative cost — each replica is a full additional copy, for real availability), then $\text{Storage}_{\text{total}} = \text{Storage}_{\text{replicated}} \times (1 + \text{overhead}_{\text{index}})$ where $\text{overhead}_{\text{index}}$ is a real, separately-stated **index/metadata overhead fraction** (e.g., a real HNSW graph's own additional structure, applied per-replica, not compounded with replication itself) — the two factors kept as two distinct, sequentially-applied real terms rather than one blended $(1+\text{overhead})$ term that would obscure which cost comes from redundancy versus which comes from the index structure itself.
- **Hand calc:** A real, worked storage-sizing calculation at a stated real corpus size and embedding dimension, computed in the three explicit real steps above (raw → replicated → total-with-index-overhead), so the real dollar/byte cost attributable to replication is shown separately from the real cost attributable to index overhead; a second, real worked (no-formula) walkthrough of a deletion-propagation scenario — a document deleted from the source, tracked through to confirmed removal from every real index replica — making the real lifecycle requirement (Module 04's own syllabus-mandated scope) concrete, not just stated.

**Module 06 — Deployment Strategies, Progressive Rollout & Experimentation Infrastructure**
- **Formula (core, canary decision rule — with an explicit monitoring-window requirement, not a threshold check alone):** A real, simple, explicit per-stage promotion rule — $\text{Promote} = (\text{ErrorRate} \leq \tau_{\text{err}}) \land (\text{p99 Latency} \leq \tau_{\text{lat}}) \land (\text{QualityScore} \geq \tau_{\text{quality}}) \land (\text{GuardrailFlagRate} \leq \tau_{\text{safety}})$ — a conjunction of real, independently-monitored thresholds, any one of which failing halts the ramp or triggers rollback. **Stated explicitly as a real, required companion condition, not an implicit assumption**: this rule is only evaluated once a real per-stage **minimum sample size** ($N_{\text{min}}$ real requests observed) and a real **minimum observation duration** ($T_{\text{min}}$, e.g., real time to capture a full traffic-pattern cycle) have both been satisfied — a stage's metrics computed from too few real requests or too short a real window are explicitly treated as *not yet decidable*, neither promoted nor rolled back, rather than a small, noisy early sample being allowed to trigger either decision.
- **Hand calc:** A real, worked canary-ramp walkthrough across 3 traffic stages (e.g., 5% → 25% → 100%), with real stated per-stage metric values evaluated against the promotion rule above only after the real stated $N_{\text{min}}$/$T_{\text{min}}$ window is satisfied — including one stage that real, deliberately fails one specific threshold (e.g., quality score dips below $\tau_{\text{quality}}$ while every other signal passes), computed to trigger a real, correct rollback decision; a second, real contrasting sub-example showing an early-stage snapshot that looks "all green" but has not yet met the real $N_{\text{min}}$ sample-size requirement, explicitly held at "not yet decidable" rather than prematurely promoted.

**Module 07 — Reliability Engineering: Redundancy, Fallbacks & Multi-Provider Architecture**
- **Formula (core, retry/backoff — with jitter, the production-critical term):** Exponential backoff with a real, stated cap **and real random jitter**, $\text{Delay}_n = \min(\text{Delay}_{\text{base}} \times 2^n + \text{Jitter}_n, \text{Delay}_{\text{max}})$, where $\text{Jitter}_n$ is real random noise (e.g., uniform over $[0, \text{Delay}_{\text{base}} \times 2^n]$, "full jitter") — stated explicitly as the term that prevents many real clients failing at the same moment from retrying in lockstep and re-synchronizing into a real thundering-herd retry burst against the already-degraded dependency; pure exponential backoff without jitter is shown as insufficient on its own for exactly this reason, not presented as a complete solution.
- **Retry eligibility (no formula, a required real taxonomy before any retry logic is designed):** Explicit real classification of which failures are retry-eligible — **transient errors** (a real, likely-temporary fault — retry), **rate limits** (retry, but real backoff should honor a provider's stated `Retry-After` signal where available), **timeouts** (retry only if the request is real, confirmed idempotent — otherwise a timeout with an unknown-completion-state real side effect must not be blindly retried), and **non-retryable errors** (a real client-side/validation fault — retrying an already-guaranteed-to-fail request only adds real load with zero chance of success) — stated as a required upfront classification, since treating all errors as uniformly retryable is itself a real reliability bug.
- **Hand calc:** A real, worked total-latency calculation across a bounded retry sequence (e.g., 3 attempts) under exponential backoff **with jitter** and a real stated cap, contrasted directly against a real, computed "naive immediate retry, no jitter" total that shows a materially worse real synchronized-load-spike risk on the already-degraded dependency — making the retry-storm risk concrete with two contrasting computed scenarios; a second, real worked (no-formula) example applying the retry-eligibility taxonomy to 4 real, distinct error scenarios (one per category above), showing the real, correct retry/no-retry decision for each; a third, real worked (no-formula) example distinguishing a primary-model response from a fallback-model response on the identical prompt, calling out a real, stated capability difference (e.g., a shorter real context window on the fallback) as the explicit trade-off accepted for availability.
