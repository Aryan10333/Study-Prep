**Notebook 01 — Real Queueing Simulation & Cost-Engineering Sweep**
- A real, live discrete-event simulation (Python, not a closed-form shortcut) of a Poisson-arrival request stream feeding a bounded-capacity multi-server queue, run for a real, large number of simulated requests — empirically measuring the real average in-flight concurrency and comparing it directly against Little's Law's predicted $L = \text{QPS} \times T_{\text{req}}$ from Module 03. **Corrected framing**: Little's Law ($L = \lambda W$) is a real mathematical identity under its applicability conditions (a stable, real steady-state system), not a relationship expected to systematically diverge at high utilization — real queuing *delay* can grow sharply as utilization approaches 1, but $L=\lambda W$ itself should still hold when $\lambda$, $L$, and $W$ are all measured consistently over the same real steady-state window. This experiment's real purpose is therefore testing **measurement accuracy and the identity's applicability conditions** (does the simulation reach a real steady state within the measurement window, are $\lambda$/$W$ measured over a consistent real time window, does a short or non-steady-state window produce a real, spurious-looking mismatch) — not hunting for a law violation that would indicate a real bug in the simulation or measurement methodology, not a real property of queueing theory.
- **$T_{\text{req}}$ defined explicitly, real service time only, not real end-to-end response time**: this notebook's simulation distinguishes real service time (the real time a request spends being actively processed once assigned a server) from real response time (service time plus real real queue-wait time before a server becomes available) — Module 03's own $T_{\text{req}}$ in the Little's Law formula must be applied consistently to whichever real quantity is actually being measured as $L$, stated explicitly before any comparison, since mixing the two would make the empirical comparison internally inconsistent.
- A real, parameterized sweep of Module 03's self-hosted-vs-API cost formulas across a wide, real range of assumed volumes and cost-basis values, extending the module's own single break-even point to a full real sensitivity analysis.
- A real, swept sensitivity analysis of the two-layer cache-savings formulas across varied real hit-rate assumptions, testing whether semantic caching's dollar-savings advantage (shown in Module 03's own worked example) holds across the real swept range or only at the specific hit rates originally chosen.
